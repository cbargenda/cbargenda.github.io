Objective

Design a condiment dispenser that can be used in restaurants or households, where twisting the cap on top turns a blade that forces the condiment out of the container. The product has four parts: the container, the cap, the connecting rod, and the blade.

My contribution and process

  • Blade: modeled with an extrude and a rectangular pattern for symmetry, using fillets instead of complex curves.
  • Cap: created with a revolve from a 2D profile, with fillets to smooth the edges.
  • Connecting rod: a cylindrical extrusion with parametric dimensions, so its length can be adjusted easily.
  • Container: given a transparent appearance so the internal parts are visible; the interior was intentionally simplified (no threads or detailed seals).

Assembly and joints

I brought the four parts into a Fusion assembly as linked components and connected them with three joints: a rigid joint from the cap to the connecting rod, a rigid joint from the rod to the blade, and a revolute joint between the blade and the bottom of the container. In the simulated assembly, twisting the cap rotates the blade.

GD&T analysis

As part of the project, I identified which geometric controls would matter if the design were produced: flatness on the cap face, position on the blade features, concentricity between the shaft, cap, and blade hub, perpendicularity of the shaft, cylindricity of the shaft, and runout.

Key choice: position on the blade features. If the blades are not positioned accurately, the assembly becomes unbalanced, which affects efficiency and can cause shaking at higher speeds.

Technical drawings

I produced a dimensioned multiview drawing with an isometric view for each part, using Fusion's drawing workspace.

Outcome and learning

The deliverable was a four-part CAD assembly with joints, four dimensioned drawings, renders, an exploded-view animation, and a final presentation. The main lesson was how keeping part geometry simple, using fillets instead of complex surfacing and a simplified container interior, made the parts easier to fit together cleanly in the assembly.

Surface modeling + rendering

Sunglasses

A sunglasses model created in Autodesk Fusion. I used T-spline surface modeling to create the curved, contoured geometry of the frame, modeled the frame, lenses, and arms as one coordinated assembly, and rendered textures and lighting to produce realistic digital product visuals.

Design exploration: I rendered the same frame in three material finishes to compare how wood grain and translucent plastic change the look of the product.

Technical drawing

GD&T Plate Drawing

I created a technical drawing of a plate part and applied geometric dimensioning and tolerancing based on a datum reference frame (A, B, C). The drawing specifies position tolerances for four holes, perpendicularity on two reference edges, flatness on the primary datum, and a profile tolerance on the curved edge.

Sheet metal + drawing

Sheet Metal Drawing

I modeled a formed sheet metal part with 12 bends and produced a technical drawing that documents it. The drawing includes a dimensioned flat pattern (6.71 × 5.21), an isometric view of the folded part, and a bend table specifying the direction, angle, and radius of each bend: six up and six down, all 90° with a 0.1 radius.

Template LLC: Company Website

I designed and built the website for my own web design business, Template LLC, which offers websites and ongoing maintenance to local small businesses. The site walks potential clients through services, a three-step process, tiered pricing, and an About section, with a free discovery call as the main call to action.

Key design decision: I organized pricing into three clearly separated tiers with a highlighted recommended plan, so business owners can compare options at a glance.

Prototype

Hydrosyst: Plumbing Website Prototype

A concept website for a plumbing company, designed to show prospective small-business clients what a modern, conversion-focused site could look like. It leads with an emergency call to action, lists services with starting prices, and includes an AI-guided emergency triage page where visitors describe a problem and receive step-by-step safety guidance.

Key design decision: I placed the Emergency button in both the top navigation and the hero section, so a visitor with a burst pipe can reach help in one click.

Prototype

Maison: Fashion Retail Website Prototype

A minimal e-commerce concept for a fashion boutique, using a restrained layout, light typography, and large product imagery to create a premium feel. The site includes a seasonal hero section, shop-by-category tiles, and a new arrivals grid with prices and color options.

Key design decision: I kept the navigation to three items and let photography carry the page, so the products stay the focus.
